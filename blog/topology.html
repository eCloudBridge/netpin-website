Mastering Microservice Topology

Understanding the complex web of interactions between your microservices is the first step toward true cloud-native observability. If you can't see it, you can't fix it.

Visualizing the Invisible

Modern applications are distributed across hundreds of containers, often deployed across multiple clusters and cloud providers. Traditional monitoring relies on tabular metrics and logs, which completely fail to convey the relationships between services. A dynamic topology map instantly reveals bottlenecks, orphaned services, and hidden dependencies.

Identifying Costly Network Hops

One of the hidden killers of cloud budgets is cross-zone and cross-region data transfer. Without a clear topology, it's incredibly easy to accidentally configure a frontend service in AWS us-east-1a to repeatedly query a backend database in us-east-1b. These network hops add milliseconds of latency to every request and significantly inflate your AWS bill.

By visualizing traffic flows, teams can optimize pod placement using Kubernetes node affinity, ensuring that chattiest microservices reside on the same underlying physical infrastructure.

Automated Discovery with Netpin

Netpin automatically discovers and maps your entire Kubernetes ecosystem in real-time, requiring zero instrumentation changes to your code. By leveraging eBPF (Extended Berkeley Packet Filter) technology, Netpin observes every network packet at the kernel level, creating a flawless, low-overhead map of your architecture.
