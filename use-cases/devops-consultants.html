Scenario 1 Discovery · Topology · IDI

Day one with an undocumented client estate

Boutique DevOps consultancy · new client: 2 EKS clusters, 40 microservices, no documentation

The situation

A new client's original platform engineer had left. There were no diagrams, Helm charts covered half the services, the rest had been applied by hand, and nobody knew which services were internet-facing. The client wanted an assessment and a plan within two weeks.

Why it hurts

  • Manual discovery consumed most of the assessment budget.
  • Findings were opinion-based without a baseline.
  • Risky areas (public services, privileged pods) were found late.

How Netpin handles it

  1. Connect read-onlyAdd the client's AWS account; EKS clusters are discovered and connected automatically.
  2. Map everythingTopology shows every workload, Service, Ingress and dependency; drift shows what was applied by hand vs Helm.
  3. BaselineIDI, compliance posture, drift and spend in one place on day one.
  4. Prioritised planFastest ways to raise the score and Fix these first become the engagement roadmap.
  5. Explain & FixEach recommendation comes with steps the client's team can follow.

Scenario 2 Multi-company · Switching

Twelve clients without twelve kubeconfigs

Managed service provider · 12 client companies · mix of EKS, AKS and on-prem clusters

The situation

Engineers kept a folder of kubeconfigs, VPN profiles and notes per client. Switching context during an incident took ten minutes, credentials expired at the worst moment, and an engineer once ran a command against the wrong client's cluster.

Why it hurts

  • Slow context switching during incidents.
  • Credential sprawl was a security risk for the MSP and its clients.
  • Mistakes across clients are reputation-ending.

How Netpin handles it

  1. A company per clientEach client is its own company in Netpin with its own projects, members and connections.
  2. Instant switchingSwitch company and project from the top bar; every page follows.
  3. Short-lived cloud credentialsManaged clusters are reached with tokens minted on demand from each client's read-only cloud identity.
  4. Role-based accessGive client staff access to their own company only.
  5. Read-only by designNetpin never writes to client infrastructure, so it cannot run the wrong command.

What changes — and what to measure

Context switch timeSeconds
Long-lived kubeconfigs stored by engineersZero for managed clusters
Cross-client mistakesImpossible from Netpin

Scenario 3 Drift · Executive Brief

Proving value — and keeping fixes fixed

DevOps agency on a monthly retainer · client CFO questioning renewal

The situation

Over three months the agency fixed resource limits, locked down RBAC and set up Helm for the remaining services. In month four the client's developers started editing deployments by hand again, undoing part of the work, and the CFO asked what the retainer was delivering.

Why it hurts

  • Work done was invisible to client leadership.
  • Regressions by the client's team erased progress.
  • Renewal conversations relied on anecdotes.

How Netpin handles it

  1. Drift guards your workHand edits that undo your changes appear as drift, attributed to who made them, within 15 minutes.
  2. Alerts to both teamsCritical drift notifies the agency and client channels.
  3. Score historyThe IDI trend shows improvement since the engagement started.
  4. Executive BriefGrades, trends and decisions in plain language for the CFO — printed straight into the monthly report.
  5. Deploy GateKeep regressions out of production with the client's own CI.
