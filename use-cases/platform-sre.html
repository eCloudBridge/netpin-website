Scenario 1 Drift · Relational impact

The shared ConfigMap that took down two services

Mid-size SaaS · 3 SRE engineers · 4 clusters · 120 workloads · Argo CD for some apps, Helm for others

The situation

A developer edited a ConfigMap to change a feature flag for the search service. The same ConfigMap was mounted by the recommendations and notifications services, which read a connection string from it — the edit had accidentally reformatted that line. Twenty minutes later both services were failing health checks. The on-call engineer spent forty minutes looking at the wrong services.

Why it hurts

  • The blast radius of a "small" change was invisible.
  • Time to identify the cause was longer than time to fix.
  • The change was made with kubectl edit, so nothing in Git explained it.

How Netpin handles it

  1. Change detectedThe ConfigMap is compared with its kubectl or Helm declaration and the edited keys are flagged as drift.
  2. Shared configurationThe Impact tab lists every workload that mounts it — Shares configuration with — so the link to the failing services is immediate.
  3. Internet-facing pathsNetpin shows which public endpoints route to the affected services.
  4. Who and whenThe field manager and timestamp identify the edit and its author.
  5. Revert in one stepA generated kubectl patch restores the declared value; or commit the new value with the adopt snippet.

Scenario 2 Alerts · Escalation

From alert storms to one alert per real problem

E-commerce platform · 24/7 on-call rotation of 6 · 400 alerts a week, 30 actionable

The situation

A flapping condition on one node pool generated hundreds of notifications overnight. The on-call engineer muted the channel to sleep and missed a genuine critical alert about a security group change two hours later. The next morning the team discovered the change had exposed an admin port.

Why it hurts

  • Alert fatigue made the important alert invisible.
  • Duplicate notifications for the same condition flooded every channel.
  • Nothing escalated when an alert was ignored.

How Netpin handles it

  1. One alert per fingerprintAlerts are fingerprinted by tenant, environment, type, resource and field — repeats increment an occurrence count instead of paging again.
  2. Cooldown and hysteresisA condition that recurs within the cooldown (5 minutes by default) is treated as flapping; numeric conditions must clear the threshold by a margin before recovering.
  3. Maintenance windowsPlanned work is recorded as suppressed and not notified.
  4. EscalationAlerts not acknowledged within the rule's timeout (30 minutes by default) escalate to critical and P1 and re-notify Slack, PagerDuty or email.
  5. ContextEvery alert links to its source — the drift finding, compliance control or Deploy Gate decision — with redacted evidence.

Scenario 3 Blast radius · Pre-flight

Knowing what depends on the database before the migration

Payments startup turned scale-up · Postgres on RDS · 14 services · quarterly engine upgrade

The situation

A planned RDS engine upgrade was scheduled for a low-traffic window. The team listed the services they believed used the database — nine. In fact fourteen did, including a reconciliation job that ran once a night. It failed silently for three days after the upgrade because its driver was incompatible.

Why it hurts

  • Dependency knowledge lived in people's heads and was out of date.
  • Silent failures in batch jobs were discovered late.
  • Risk reviews relied on spreadsheets.

How Netpin handles it

  1. Discovered dependenciesTopology maps which workloads connect to which services and resources, refreshed with every discovery.
  2. Blast radius scoreThe database's score combines reach, dependency centrality, shared-infrastructure and criticality factors, ×1.25 in production — here, critical.
  3. Full listAffected workloads, services, namespaces, clusters and accounts, with the dependency paths.
  4. Pre-flight riskBefore the change, pre-flight shows the risk score, contributing factors and confidence.
  5. Explain & FixVerification steps for each affected service after the change.

Questions teams like yours ask

Does drift detection need an agent?

No. Netpin reads through the Kubernetes and cloud APIs with read-only permissions. An optional lightweight agent exists for environments that prefer push-based collection.

Will autoscalers create noise?

No. Replica counts managed by an HPA, server-populated fields and defaults are excluded; only fields you declared are compared.

Can we see pod logs?

Yes — open a pod in Topology to see its recent log lines next to its health, findings and dependants.
