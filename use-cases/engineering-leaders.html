Scenario 1 Executive Brief · Decisions

The quarterly board update that used to take a week

Series C SaaS · VP Engineering reporting to the board · 9 teams · AWS + GCP

The situation

Each quarter the VP of Engineering assembled a board update from six tools: an uptime report, a security scanner export, a spreadsheet of audit actions, the cloud bill and release statistics from CI. It took a week, the numbers did not reconcile, and the board still asked "so are we in good shape?"

Why it hurts

  • Leadership time spent on assembling data, not on decisions.
  • Inconsistent numbers undermined credibility.
  • No clear asks — the update informed but did not drive action.

How Netpin handles it

  1. Overall gradeA to F, with a one-paragraph verdict anyone can read.
  2. Five scorecardsPlatform health (IDI), security risk, audit readiness, cost efficiency and delivery — each with its change over the period.
  3. Decisions neededRanked "now" and "this quarter": close critical exposures, raise readiness for the weakest framework, commit to steady usage, stabilise releases — each with impact and owner.
  4. Where the risk isControls grouped into business areas such as access, internet exposure, data protection and supply chain.
  5. Print / PDFA clean board-pack page with what the numbers are based on.

What changes — and what to measure

Time to prepare the board updateMinutes
Decisions with an ownerEvery "now" item assigned
Grade trendQuarter over quarter

Scenario 2 Cost · Forecast · Savings

The cloud bill finance cannot explain

Marketplace scale-up · $180k/month across AWS and GCP · finance asking for a 20% reduction

The situation

Finance asked engineering to cut cloud spend by 20%. The first list of ideas added up to 45% — because the same instances appeared in rightsizing, Graviton migration and Savings Plan recommendations, each claiming the full saving. When the changes were made, the actual saving was 11%, and trust between finance and engineering suffered.

Why it hurts

  • Double-counted savings set unrealistic expectations.
  • Spend could not be attributed to clusters, teams or integrations.
  • Month-end surprises with no early warning.

How Netpin handles it

  1. Calibrated to the billResource prices are scaled to match billed spend, covering Savings Plans, RIs and credits; the basis is shown on every number.
  2. No double countingSavings apply in order — remove waste, modernise, schedule, commit — each on the cost left by the previous step.
  3. AttributionCost by cloud account, cluster, workload, Helm release, GitOps application, repository and CI server.
  4. ForecastMonth-end forecast with a 95% range and anomaly detection.
  5. Executive viewCost efficiency grade and the "commit" and "remove idle" decisions in the Executive Brief.

What changes — and what to measure

Promised vs realised savingsShould match
Unexplained billed spendShown separately, not hidden
Forecast vs actualWithin the 95% range

Scenario 3 IDI · Prioritisation

Funding reliability work with evidence

Media company · 12 product teams · platform team asking for two more engineers

The situation

The platform team asked for headcount to pay down infrastructure debt. Leadership asked what it would change. The team could describe problems — missing limits, inconsistent probes, manual changes, failing controls — but not quantify them or show progress.

Why it hurts

  • Investment requests competed with features on anecdotes.
  • No baseline to judge whether last year's investment worked.
  • Teams could not see how their work moved the needle.

How Netpin handles it

  1. BaselineEach team's IDI and where its points are lost.
  2. Biggest lever"Infrastructure health costs 54% of lost points; raising it by 10 adds 4 IDI points."
  3. Fix planRanked actions with estimated IDI gain.
  4. ForecastWhere the score is heading without action.
  5. ProgressScore history per team and the grade trend in the Executive Brief.

What changes — and what to measure

IDI per teamBefore and after the investment
Teams in warning or criticalDown each quarter
Manual changesDown as GitOps adoption rises
